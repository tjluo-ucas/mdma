## Course purpose

MDMA serves learners who use quantitative models to support management action. It begins with problem structuring and cash-flow evaluation, advances through optimization, linear/integer programming, network flow, and Monte Carlo simulation, and closes with a decision brief. Software proficiency is not the endpoint; the transferable discipline is to define the decision, expose assumptions, represent trade-offs, seek evidence, challenge results, and own action.

The revision adds three **ungraded frontier lenses**: robust/stochastic decisions, data-driven prescriptive analytics, and model governance with AI. They challenge classical assumptions without silently changing the approved 2026 modules, cases, or grading policy.

## Problem-oriented learning loop

1. **Encounter:** read an incomplete or contested business situation before receiving a method label;
2. **Activate and frame:** separate facts, assumptions, unknowns, stakeholders, owner, and horizon;
3. **Form learning issues:** identify knowledge, data, and clarifications needed;
4. **Build alternatives:** compare at least two structures or strategies;
5. **Solve and challenge:** choose a spreadsheet, Python, analytical, or professional-solver route, then cross-check with a hand case, alternative implementation, residuals, boundaries, sensitivity, infeasibility, and counterexamples;
6. **Recommend:** state action, thresholds, risks, and exceptions in a one-page decision brief;
7. **Reflect and transfer:** explain what changed and apply the method in a new context.

The instructor supplies scaffolds, counterexamples, and gates, then progressively transfers problem definition, model choice, and verification responsibility from D0 to D5.

## Learning outcomes

Students completing the course should be able to:

- translate a business narrative into decisions, objective, constraints, assumptions, and success measures;
- evaluate investments and acquisitions with DCF, NPV, IRR, and sensitivity analysis;
- formulate linear, nonlinear, network-flow, and integer models with a spreadsheet, Python, or professional solver appropriate to scale;
- inspect units, boundaries, domains, feasibility, optimality, and business meaning;
- use Monte Carlo simulation for demand, cancellation, and revenue risk with uncertainty intervals;
- distinguish data, structural, parameter, and implementation risk and compare nominal, scenario, and robust choices;
- distinguish prediction accuracy from decision quality and identify limits of data-driven optimization;
- compare at least two solution routes or structures and explain differences and validity boundaries;
- make an actionable recommendation with owners, risks, exceptions, and human decision boundaries.

## Prerequisites

Calculus, probability and statistics, and basic computing. Spreadsheet or Python experience is welcome; prior Solver, optimization-library, or simulation-add-in experience is not required. Tool on-ramps are provided, while students must read English-language cases, audit formulas, and contribute to group modeling and presentation.

## Case–experiment architecture

- **Five group exercises** build intuition for present value, integer choice, EOQ, cash-flow matching, and target probability;
- **six formal cases** cover acquisition, pricing, product mix, logistics, capital expansion, and revenue management;
- each formal case includes a **Reality Lab** varying a parameter, structure, objective, evidence source, or implementation constraint;
- every experiment includes a comparison such as hand versus code, Excel versus Python, naive versus optimized, or deterministic versus stochastic.

## Approved assessment baseline

| Component | Weight | Rule |
|---|---:|---|
| Group case modeling and analysis | 75% | C1 practice; choose three from C2–C6, 25 points each |
| Individual reflection | 10% | One page on learning and workplace application, due June 30 |
| Individual team contribution | 10% | Peer rating with rubric, appeal, and instructor review |
| Class participation | 5% | Questions, answers, presentations; not login time |

Late/resubmitted/extra/missing work, membership changes, and the common rubric require final approval. An LLM may not select a student's three graded cases.

## AI and academic integrity

An AI agent may ask about variables, units, constraints, and missing data, then propose models, code, checks, and counterexamples. It may not claim a Solver, Python, or simulation run unless execution, inputs, environment, versions, and logs exist. Graded cases use hint budgets and a solution embargo; complete workbooks, complete code, or highly isomorphic reports are prohibited before the deadline. Students explain the model and independently verify critical results with a small case, test, or second implementation.
