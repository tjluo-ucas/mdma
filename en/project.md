## Deterministic 100-point structure

| Component | Points | Responsibility and boundary |
|---|---:|---|
| Three group cases selected from C2–C6 | 75 | C1 excluded; student selects; 25 each |
| One-page individual reflection | 10 | Learning, transfer, limits, next step; separate from group report |
| Individual team contribution | 10 | Peer evidence, rubric, appeal, instructor review |
| Class participation | 5 | Useful questions, answers, presentations, and feedback |

The system versions policy and explicitly handles mistaken C1, extra, missing, resubmitted, late, and membership-changing work. Login duration, word count, writing style, and inferred personality cannot substitute for contribution or participation evidence.

## Proposed case rubric (instructor approval pending)

| Dimension | Proposed share | Inspectable evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Problem structure and assumptions | 20% | Owner, objective, boundary, variables, units, assumptions, unknowns |
| Model correctness | 20% | Formula, objective, constraints, random process, domains, tool mapping |
| Verification and reproducibility | 20% | Checks, boundaries, status, version/seed, rerun |
| Reality Lab and method comparison | 25% | Drivers, counterexamples, thresholds, variants, alternative methods, limits |
| Recommendation and communication | 15% | Actionable, evidence-aligned, exceptions, accountability |

Freeze the rubric before submission. A change creates a new version; it cannot silently alter completed grading.

**Tool neutrality:** Excel, Python, analytical derivation, and professional solvers earn no automatic premium or penalty. An opaque complex tool cannot compensate for a wrong question; a simple rigorous method can earn the same highest evaluation.

## Individual transfer prompts

1. Which real workplace decision deserves a model, and who owns it?
2. Which implicit assumptions, spreadsheets, or rules currently drive it?
3. What smallest model could create insight, and what should remain outside the model?
4. How will it be validated, monitored, overridden, and reviewed?
5. What may AI assist, and which responsibilities must people retain?

## D0–D5 progression

| Stage | Evidence |
|---|---|
| D0–D1 | Distinguish inputs, decisions, objective, constraints, output |
| D1–D2 | Correctly model in any tool and pass a hand-sized case |
| D2–D3 | Independently formalize a narrative and explain status, constraints, and drivers |
| D3–D4 | Compare methods and reality variants; complete sensitivity/simulation, infeasibility diagnosis, and counterexamples |
| D5 | Select and compare methods, then rebuild, verify, implement, and govern a model in unfamiliar business |

<div class="notice"><strong>Human final responsibility</strong>Final grades, case-data repair, solution release, integrity findings, contribution disputes, and anomalous results remain instructor decisions rather than AI rulings.</div>
