<section class="section">
  <div class="section-head"><p class="kicker">Authoritative definition</p><h2>What is MDMA as a discipline?</h2><p>MDMA is this course's abbreviation for <strong>Management Decision Modeling &amp; Analysis</strong>, not a universally standardized standalone discipline name. Its foundations join Operations Research, Management Science, Analytics, and Decision Analysis. <a href="https://www.informs.org/Explore/Operations-Research-Analytics">INFORMS</a> emphasizes scientific and mathematical methods that turn complex problems and data into better decisions. The <a href="https://connect.informs.org/das/about-us/about">INFORMS Decision Analysis Society</a> adds decisions under uncertainty, multiple objectives, risk, behavior, and group processes.</p></div>
  <div class="layer-grid">
    <article class="layer-card"><span class="number">01</span><h3>Frame</h3><p>Name the decision owner, actions, objectives, horizon, stakeholders, and success criteria.</p></article>
    <article class="layer-card"><span class="number">02</span><h3>Represent</h3><p>Abstract reality into data, variables, objectives, constraints, relationships, uncertainty, and assumptions.</p></article>
    <article class="layer-card"><span class="number">03</span><h3>Solve</h3><p>Use DCF, optimization, simulation, or decision analysis to create comparable actions.</p></article>
    <article class="layer-card"><span class="number">04</span><h3>Challenge</h3><p>Test residuals, feasibility, gaps, sensitivity, counterexamples, bias, and the model–reality gap.</p></article>
    <article class="layer-card"><span class="number">05</span><h3>Act</h3><p>Explain trade-offs, thresholds, risks, implementation, overrides, ownership, and review measures.</p></article>
  </div>
  <div class="notice"><strong>Boundary</strong>A model is not reality, and a solver is not the decision maker. “Optimal” describes a mathematical status under the model, data, objective, constraints, and tolerances—not automatically the best real-world action.</div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="section-head"><p class="kicker">Agent-era difficulty</p><h2>Why is it still hard with Codex?</h2><p><a href="https://developers.openai.com/">Official OpenAI documentation</a> says Codex can understand codebases, build and test features, fix bugs, and review changes. In MDMA it can rapidly draft candidate models, Python/solver code, scenarios, and tests. The hard work shifts toward problem structure, value judgments, and responsibility for evidence.</p></div>
  <div class="card-grid">
    <article class="info-card"><span class="tag">Values</span><h3>An objective is not a fact</h3><p>Profit, service, equity, safety, and long-term capability conflict. People must explain whose values enter the model.</p></article>
    <article class="info-card"><span class="tag">Reality</span><h3>Constraints and data may be wrong</h3><p>Missing resources, wrong units, optimistic demand, or unimplementable policy produce precisely solved wrong answers.</p></article>
    <article class="info-card"><span class="tag">Uncertainty</span><h3>The future is not one parameter table</h3><p>Dependence, tail risk, regime change, and competitor response require scenarios, simulation, robustness, and judgment.</p></article>
    <article class="info-card"><span class="tag">Accountability</span><h3>A recommendation must survive challenge</h3><p>Managers need thresholds, exceptions, near-optimal alternatives, affected groups, and stop conditions—not copied agent prose.</p></article>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="section-head"><p class="kicker">Our pedagogy</p><h2>Start from contested problems; improve decision quality through counterexamples</h2><p>The course evaluates a way of thinking—not spreadsheet menus, Python line count, or solver brands.</p></div>
  <div class="track-grid">
    <article class="track-card"><span class="tag">Problem loop</span><h3>Frame before formalizing</h3><ul><li>Separate facts, assumptions, unknowns, actions, and stakeholders</li><li>Draw a timeline, influence diagram, or network before equations</li><li>Compare at least two model structures or actions</li></ul></article>
    <article class="track-card"><span class="tag">Evidence loop</span><h3>Falsify before recommending</h3><ul><li>Cross-check with a small truth case, second implementation, and naive policy</li><li>Test infeasibility, extremes, sensitivity, and recommendation reversals</li><li>Use a Decision Brief to state action, risk, override, and next evidence</li></ul></article>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="section-head"><p class="kicker">AI_Tutoring</p><h2>Novice to expert: less scaffolding, greater decision responsibility</h2><p>AI_Tutoring advances learners through independent attempts, model validation, business explanation, counterexamples, and transfer evidence—not similarity to a standard answer or agent confidence.</p></div>
  <div class="card-grid">
    <article class="info-card"><span class="tag">D0 · Diagnose</span><h3>Recognize decision language</h3><p>Separate inputs, decisions, objectives, constraints, risks, and units; diagnose mathematical, probabilistic, and domain prerequisites.</p></article>
    <article class="info-card"><span class="tag">D1 · Reproduce</span><h3>Guided reproduction</h3><p>Rerun a small DCF, LP, or simulation and explain every formula, status, and business meaning.</p></article>
    <article class="info-card"><span class="tag">D2 · Apply</span><h3>Guided variation</h3><p>Change one parameter, constraint, or objective while the Tutor supplies only interfaces, milestones, and checks.</p></article>
    <article class="info-card"><span class="tag">D3 · Analyze</span><h3>Independent modeling</h3><p>Select structure and tools from a case narrative, compare alternatives, and deliver a reproducible package.</p></article>
    <article class="info-card"><span class="tag">D4 · Evaluate</span><h3>Evaluate trade-offs</h3><p>Handle multiple objectives, uncertainty, infeasibility, robustness, near-optimality, and implementation risk.</p></article>
    <article class="info-card"><span class="tag">D5 · Transfer</span><h3>Create and transfer</h3><p>Rebuild the problem–model–evidence–action loop in an unfamiliar business and defend it under review.</p></article>
  </div>
  <div class="notice"><strong>The Tutor is not an autopilot</strong>Students attempt first. The Hint Budget rises from Socratic questions and evidence gaps to method advice without releasing graded-case answers. Exhausted hints, model conflict, and high-risk advice escalate to a teacher or TA. Final grading and solution embargoes remain human decisions.</div>
</section>
